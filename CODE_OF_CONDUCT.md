# Code of conduct

CyberCheck is a small project run by volunteers who want fewer people to lose money to phishing. We want
everyone who takes part, whether they write code, report a false positive or ask a question, to be
treated with respect.

## What we expect

- Be kind and patient, especially with people who are new to open source or to security.
- Assume good intent, and ask before you assume someone is wrong.
- Criticise code, ideas and decisions, not people. Explain why, and suggest something better when you
  can.
- Accept that a maintainer may say no to a change, and that reasons will be given.
- Keep discussions on topic and in public where possible, so others can learn from them.
- Respect people's privacy. Do not share anyone's personal details, private messages or identifying
  information without their permission.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, threats, or deliberate intimidation, in public or in private.
- Discriminatory jokes or language, or unwelcome sexual attention, about anyone's identity, background,
  body or abilities.
- Posting other people's personal information, including information about phishing victims or domain
  registrants.
- Using this project, its issue tracker or its data to target, attack or embarrass people or
  organisations.
- Persistently disrupting discussions, or continuing after you have been asked to stop.

## Scope

This applies in the repository (issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews and commits) and anywhere
you represent the project, such as when you discuss it in other communities or send reports produced
with it.

## Reporting a problem

If someone's behaviour makes you uncomfortable, or you see something that breaks this code, tell the
maintainers of https://github.com/AR13X3/cybercheck:

- For a public, low-stakes matter, you can comment or open an issue.
- For anything you would rather keep private, use the repository's private reporting form
  (https://github.com/AR13X3/cybercheck/security/advisories/new) and start the title with "Conduct:".
  Only the maintainers can see it. We will not treat it as a security report, and we will not share who
  reported it without your consent.
- For content that breaks GitHub's own rules, you can also use GitHub's "Report content" option.

We will read every report, respond as soon as we can, and act in proportion: from a private word,
through removing comments, to blocking someone from the project. If a maintainer is involved in the
problem, they will step back from handling it.
